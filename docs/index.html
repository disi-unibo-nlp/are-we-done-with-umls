Are We Done with UMLS Injection into Language Models for Medical Question Answering?

Maybe not!
    Department of Computer Science and Engineering, University of Bologna, Italy All authors contributed equally.
    Paper Code and annotations Citation Play the 8-bit game

    To answer medical exam questions more reliably, language models are often given UMLS, the largest curated map of medical terminology. The reported gains are small, and nobody had checked whether they come from UMLS at all. We reviewed every study from 2020 to 2025, 25 in total, and rebuilt their choices one at a time.

    Inside UMLS

    UMLS, the Unified Medical Language System of the US National Library of Medicine, is a database that joins the main medical vocabularies under shared concept IDs. Pick a table to see the rows that describe one disease, esophageal achalasia.

    What we did

    We searched Scopus for work on knowledge graphs and language models for question answering and reasoning, published from 2020 to 2025. We kept the studies where UMLS plays a substantive role, the task is medical question answering or a closely related reasoning task, and the method is described well enough to compare. Two medical NLP researchers annotated every study independently, with 90.2% agreement.

    Then we tested the choices these studies make, one at a time and with everything else fixed: two entity linkers, five UMLS releases and vocabulary subsets, retrieval depth and connectivity, retraining of QA-GNN and GreaseLM, and ten modern language models reading UMLS evidence as text.

    What to report with every UMLS result

    Each of these choices changes the UMLS evidence that reaches the model. Reporting all nine is the minimum for a gain to be traceable to UMLS.
