Maybe not!

We examine how UMLS construction changes the biomedical evidence available for medical question answering. Systems select a release and source vocabularies, link input text to concepts, then retrieve and expose part of the resource to a language model. We vary these choices while holding the remaining pipeline fixed.

  • NEL · named entity linking
  • KSR · knowledge subgraph retrieval
  • KI · knowledge injection

A review of 25 studies published between 2020 and 2025, complemented by controlled linking, retrieval and reader experiments. Two researchers independently annotated the methods, reaching 90.2% agreement before adjudication. 11 studies released code (44%), and 6 of 25 (24%) were operationally reproducible under the review protocol.

The resource

Most systems use names and relations

The Metathesaurus integrates biomedical vocabularies through shared concept identifiers. Its records carry names, relations, definitions, semantic types, hierarchies and attributes. 88% of the reviewed studies convert UMLS into a knowledge graph, with use concentrated on names and relations.

Release and source selection change the available knowledge

84% of reviewed publications omit the UMLS release identifier. The four reporting papers use four different releases. Source consolidation and concept retirement change the concepts and vocabularies available across the seven selected snapshots.

01 · Named entity linking

Linker choice changes answer coverage

Both MetaMap and scispaCy ground every MedQA test question stem. MetaMap reaches a concept from the correct answer in 98.2% of items and from every option in 96.4%, compared with 86.1% and 65.0% for scispaCy. Answer grounding determines which candidates can participate in question-to-answer paths.

In a manual UMLS 2023AA audit of 40 MedQA answer options, 35.6% of MetaMap's links were correct, compared with 46.7% of scispaCy's. MetaMap grounded a correct concept in all 40 options, compared with 36 for scispaCy. Its additional mappings extend option coverage while also introducing more generic or incorrect concepts.

Changing the resource with the linker held fixed

Keeping scispaCy's mention detector fixed and switching its concept knowledge base from 2020AA to 2021AA raises mean question-side concepts from 57.67 to 71.76 on MedQA development questions and from 68.46 to 78.77 on PubMedQA. Under 2023AA, restricting UMLS to DrugBank and the Disease Database reduces these means from 67.53 to 0.99 and from 76.46 to 0.96, respectively.

Answer-side coverage in released pipelines

The released QA-GNN, GREASELM and DRAGON artifacts record question-side and answer-side linking outcomes before inference. Each of the 1,273 MedQA test questions contributes four option records, for 5,092 records. These records identify which question-option pairs retain seeds for retrieval.

02 · Knowledge subgraph retrieval

Connectivity and pruning shape the retrieved graph

On PubMedQA, the mean full-UMLS neighborhood grows from 3,755 nodes at two hops to 1,336,309 at five hops. Over the same depths, the DRAGON-filtered graph grows from 193 to 46,719 nodes.

Connectivity also changes the retrieved graph. On MedQA at two hops, pooling all linked concepts yields 87 times more nodes than keeping question-to-answer paths under full UMLS, and 60 times more under the DRAGON-filtered graph. Pooled expansion admits connections within either side of the question-answer pair.

QA-GNN, GREASELM and DRAGON cap the graph at 200 nodes. Their pre-pruning graphs exceed that budget in 70 to 73% of evaluation instances, with about 1.5 answer-side concepts surviving per question-option pair. Pruning compounds the answer-side losses introduced during linking.

When linking yields zero seeds, released systems insert fixed concepts such as Serine and Ethanol. In three-seed QA-GNN comparisons, using a random CUI or skipping retrieval produces similar mean accuracy to these placeholders.

03 · Knowledge injection

Construction choices affect systems differently

We group the reviewed injection mechanisms into textual linearization through serialized facts, structural injection through graph representations at inference, and parameter encoding through training supervision. Systems are classified by their primary route.

Graph variants produce similar mean accuracy

We retrain QA-GNN and GREASELM on MedQA under seven UMLS constructions, keeping their published architectures, hyperparameters and training schedules. Each linker is paired with a graph from the same release, and each construction is evaluated over three random seeds.

QA-GNN test accuracy spans 37.5 to 38.7% across six graph configurations, compared with 37.4% for its placeholder-only control. GREASELM spans 37.7 to 39.3%, compared with 36.7% for its control. QA-GNN's graph and placeholder-only means remain close, while GREASELM's graph configurations have higher mean accuracy than its control.

Preferred evidence views differ across benchmarks

Metadata-enriched paths recover the most baseline errors for most MedQA and MedMCQA readers. Relation neighborhoods lead on nine of ten PubMedQA readers. MedQA and MedMCQA each have three evidence views, while the PubMedQA panel compares relation neighborhoods with metadata-enriched evidence.

Recovery is the percentage of a reader's zero-shot errors corrected after adding evidence. Each reader is evaluated on its own baseline-wrong subset.

A chain-of-thought pilot on a MedQA subsample changes the winning evidence view relative to answer-only prompting.

Preferred path depth varies with the reader

Within the Qwen3.5 scale ladder, the 9B reader peaks at three hops and the 35B reader at four. The comparison varies path depth on MedQA items where all three lengths are available and every candidate answer is reachable at each depth.

Reporting UMLS construction

For each result, record the selected resource, linked concepts, retrieved evidence and model input.

UMLS snapshot

  • Release version. Identify the source snapshot.
  • Subset. Name source vocabularies and concept/relation filters.
  • Metadata. Specify attached fields, including definitions and semantic types.

Named entity linking

  • Linker. Report the tool and non-default settings.
  • Linking result. Report entity coverage and unlinked items.

Knowledge subgraph retrieval

  • Connectivity rule. State which concepts are connected.
  • Depth and scale. Report hop depth and resulting subgraph size.
  • Pruning. State the criterion and node budget.
  • Fallback. Explain what happens when no seed is linked.

Review protocol

The Scopus search ran on January 15, 2026 and covered English-language conference and journal work published from 2020 to 2025. Two researchers independently annotated the screened literature and resolved disagreements through discussion.

  • UMLS must play a substantive technical role.
  • The task must be medical question answering or a related reasoning setting.
  • The method must support comparison across knowledge grounding, retrieval and injection.

Annotations draw first on the paper and supplement, then released code, then code from cited systems. The records label missing fields as unreported.
